IH2662 WEEK 3 - SILICON BASELINE
================================
Folder: C:\Users\oksan\IH2662\week3

WHAT YOU ARE DOING
Turn your 600 V hand calculation into a 2D silicon p-n junction,
increase reverse voltage, and measure the maximum electric field.
The crossing of 262,000 V/cm is a FIELD-CRITERION ESTIMATE.
This model does not include avalanche impact ionization.

STEP 1 - REVIEW YOUR INPUTS
Open parameters.json in Notepad. You do not need to change it initially.
Hand-calculation inputs:
  Donor doping:                 3.67e14 cm^-3
  Critical field:               2.62e5 V/cm
  Ideal target:                 600 V
  Depletion width at breakdown: 45.9 um
  Silicon relative permittivity:11.7
  Electron mobility:            1420 cm^2/(V s)

Additional, provisional modelling choices (not supplied by your hand sheet):
  Temperature: 300 K.
  Gaussian p diffusion with peak acceptor doping 1e17 cm^-3.
  Junction depth: 3 um; flat junction from x=0 to x=40 um, then a
  quarter-circle edge of radius 3 um, meeting the top at x=43 um.
  Top anode covers x=0 to x=38 um, entirely inside the p region.
  Width: 120 um. Total thickness: 63 um.
  Drift thickness below flat junction: 60 um.
  60 um is deliberately larger than the 45.9 um depletion width to avoid
  forcing punch-through at the analytical breakdown point. It is a
  documented simulation choice, NOT a new hand-calculation result.
  Bottom n contact is ohmic; no n+ substrate is explicitly modelled.
  Uncontacted boundaries use zero normal flux. Left side is a symmetry
  boundary; top outside the anode and far right have no oxide/surface charge.
  No guard ring, field plate, surface traps or impact ionization.
  Constant hole mobility 450 cm^2/(V s), ni=1e10 cm^-3,
  SRH lifetimes 10 us. These are assumptions requiring sources before report.
  Keep these choices fixed when comparing termination structures.

The acceptor profile is:
  NA(x,y)=NApeak*exp(-(y^2+max(x-40um,0)^2)/L^2)
  L=3um/sqrt(ln(NApeak/ND)).
Thus ND-NA=0 lies exactly on the stated junction.
The smooth diffusion differs from the ideal abrupt hand-calculation model.

STEP 2 - GENERATE AND INSPECT THE MESH
Menu option 1, or:
  .\Run-Week3.cmd mesh
Then menu option 2, or:
  .\Run-Week3.cmd view-mesh

Gmsh opens the NEW silicon mesh, not the old MOSFET.
Coordinates in Gmsh are CENTIMETRES: 0.012 cm = 120 um.
Look for a rectangular silicon region and small triangles around the
junction at 3 um depth and its curved edge. Close Gmsh when finished.
The generated .geo and .msh are in results\baseline_double_h1.
Edit parameters.json to change geometry; generated .geo files are overwritten.

STEP 3 - SOLVE AT ZERO BIAS
Menu option 3, or:
  .\Run-Week3.cmd equilibrium
Open:
  results\baseline_double_h1\mesh.png
  results\baseline_double_h1\equilibrium_field.png

A built-in junction field exists even at zero applied voltage.
Check that the red/cyan junction contour is in the intended location.
The mesh plot is in micrometres; depth increases downwards.

STEP 4 - RUN REVERSE BIAS
Menu option 4, or:
  .\Run-Week3.cmd run

Cathode remains at 0 V. Anode is ramped negative.
The plotted voltage is the POSITIVE magnitude of that reverse bias.
Each BIAS line reports voltage, peak field and peak position.
Steps reduce near the critical field; failed steps are retried smaller.
The sweep stops once the critical field is crossed, or reports an error.
A saved CSV alone does not mean the whole run passed: check for PASS.

Open results\baseline_double_h1:
  peak_field.png       YOUR MAIN WEEK 3 PLOT
  peak_field.csv       bias points and peak positions
  final_field.png      field map at the last simulated bias
  final.vtu            ParaView dataset at the last bias
  summary.json         interpolated criterion voltage and mesh size
  parameters_used.json snapshot of input values

The final field map is at the upper crossing bracket, not precisely the
interpolated criterion voltage. See summary.json and the CSV for values.
Electric field is calculated from BOTH components of -grad(Potential)
on each triangle, not from voltage itself or one edge projection.

STEP 5 - VIEW IN PARAVIEW
Double-click Open-Week3-ParaView.cmd.
If needed: select final.vtu in Pipeline Browser and click Apply.
Choose ElectricField_V_cm in the coloring dropdown.
Choose Surface With Edges to inspect the mesh.
Use the +Z view and Rescale to Data Range.
The exported geometry is in MICROMETRES and field values are V/cm.
Potential_V is voltage; it is not electric field.
The most intense field should be examined near the curved junction edge.

STEP 6 - CHECK THE RESULT BEFORE TRUSTING IT
A. Flat control: menu option 5, or:
     .\Run-Week3.cmd run --planar
   Results: results\planar_double_h1
   This extends the p diffusion across the whole width and removes the
   junction edge. Compare its threshold with the roughly 600 V ideal.
   The Gaussian junction is not exactly the abrupt analytical junction.
   If the discrepancy exceeds about 10%, investigate before continuing.
   The real edge baseline may be substantially below 600 V: that is
   not automatically a solver error.

B. Mesh check: menu option 6, or:
     .\Run-Week3.cmd run --mesh-scale 0.7
   Results: results\baseline_double_h0.7
   Compare criterion_voltage_V with baseline_double_h1.
   A small change is encouraging; one pair is not a complete convergence
   study. If the difference is more than a few percent, refine further.
   For another refinement:
     .\Run-Week3.cmd run --mesh-scale 0.5

C. Later, check lateral width/contact placement and surface assumptions.
   The chosen finite domain must not control the answer.

NUMERICAL SETUP NOTES
The initial local mesh target near the junction is 0.30 um; the 0.7
refinement run uses 0.21 um. The planar control is a 10 um wide strip with
insulating sides and a laterally uniform junction. Its width does not
represent a junction edge.
The script uses DEVSIM's bundled UMFPACK backend in standard precision;
the installed MKL Pardiso backend was unreliable for this model.
No changes to the original Week 1 launchers were needed.
The reverse ramp starts at 0.1 V. A continuation predictor uses the last
two successful solutions; steps grow up to 10 V and shrink near crossing.
Runs may take several minutes. Leave the terminal open until PASS.
For a slower high-precision reference, add --precision extended.
That writes to baseline_extended_h1, keeping the standard run separate.
See VALIDATION.json for the completed initial checks and their scope.
To re-check all three completed cases:
  ..\ih2662.cmd check_results.py
Solver reference: https://devsim.net/solver.html
